Cadence Next Product Direction

The best next move is not another isolated meeting-note feature. Cadence should become the private native capture layer for spoken work on the Mac, starting with a trust-first meeting recorder that users believe before they admire.

Codebase Context

Grounding Context

Cadence is a native macOS SwiftUI/AppKit app with two product surfaces inside one shell. The original surface is fast local dictation: capture microphone audio, transcribe locally with WhisperKit, and insert text into the focused app. The newer surface is durable meeting capture: system or microphone audio, live draft transcript, saved CAF audio, final-pass transcription, summaries, Ask/Reports, transcript history, and Markdown export.

The codebase already has serious pieces: ScreenCaptureKit system audio, Google OAuth and Calendar fetching, local JSON meeting stores, local CAF audio, Keychain token storage, contextual Screen Recording permission, transcript segment origin and recording IDs, note-local summaries, global Ask/search primitives, and privacy-safe analytics.

The most important product-risk finding is reliability. Cadence writes meeting audio during recording, but recording metadata is appended to the note only after stop. That means a force-quit during active recording can leave a CAF file that is not discoverable from the meeting note. For a meeting product, this is more damaging than a weak summary or a rough transcript.

External research changes the positioning. Bot-free capture is now table stakes across the category. Granola's defensible pattern is not just recording, but the AI notepad workflow around rough notes, calendar context, and after-meeting synthesis. MacWhisper is close local/private prior art. Cadence's sharper wedge is trust: local-first storage, visible recording state, recoverable audio, editable speaker truth, and no cloud sharing default.

Idea 1. Crash-First Meeting Ledger

Top pick
Confidence 92% Complexity Medium Reliability
Description
Make a meeting recording a durable ledger from the first audio frame, not a note artifact that becomes real only after stop. If Cadence heard it, Cadence should be able to find it, reopen it, retry finalization, and explain what survived after a crash or interruption.
Axis
Capture reliability and transcription quality.
Basis
direct: The repo evidence shows meeting audio is written incrementally, while recording metadata is appended only after stop; active-recording crash recovery therefore still needs incremental metadata or another discoverability mechanism. external: bot-free capture is now common, so trust and recovery are stronger differentiators than basic recording.
Rationale
Users forgive a mediocre transcript faster than they forgive a lost meeting. This is the foundation every other meeting feature depends on.
Downsides
It is less flashy than speaker diarization or AI summaries, and it touches storage, recovery, capture state, and UI. It also forces careful migration thinking around partial or corrupted recordings.
2

Idea 2. Meeting Runway

Confidence 76% Complexity Medium Workflow
Description
Narrow the calendar idea to a menu-bar pre-meeting cockpit: next meeting, readiness, join link, and one action to join plus record. Avoid overclaiming a full "capture OS" for now; the product move is to make the 30 seconds before a meeting calmer and more reliable.
Axis
Meeting workflow and calendar-to-capture flow.
Basis
direct: Cadence already has Google Calendar events, meeting candidate detection, note preparation, join/open behavior, and capture start coordination. external: MeetingBar and Raycast show that next-meeting menu-bar state is a natural macOS pattern.
Rationale
The user is most motivated right before a meeting. If Cadence becomes the quickest path from "meeting starts now" to "capture is rolling," it becomes a habit instead of a utility buried in the main window.
Downsides
The verifier was right that "Calendar as Capture OS" overreaches. This should stay tight: next meeting, readiness, join, record. Recurring rules and post-meeting automation can wait.
3

Idea 3. Private AI Notepad

Confidence 86% Complexity High Knowledge
Description
Make the meeting note the primary object, not the transcript. Rough notes, calendar context, live draft, final transcript, summary, Ask, and reports should cooperate around one editable working note that starts useful during the meeting and sharpens afterward.
Axis
Knowledge layer after capture.
Basis
direct: Cadence already has note bodies, summaries, Ask, Reports, transcript search/collapse, and calendar-seeded note templates. external: Granola's strongest workflow is rough human notes plus AI enhancement, not the recorder itself.
Rationale
This moves Cadence out of "private Otter alternative" territory and into "private meeting thinking surface." It also makes imperfect transcripts less fatal, because the user gets value from the working note before the final pass is perfect.
Downsides
It is a product-design commitment, not a small UI tweak. It will expose weaknesses in summary quality and note layout if the underlying capture state is not reliable.
4

Idea 4. Editable Speaker Ledger

Confidence 84% Complexity High Speakers
Description
Design speaker identity as editable meeting metadata. Start with source-aware turns, quick rename, merge/split, durable corrected names, and visible confidence rather than promising perfect automatic identity.
Axis
Knowledge layer after capture.
Basis
direct: Transcript segments already carry speaker, capture source, origin, and recording ID, but current speaker labels are capture-source proxies rather than true diarization. external: MacWhisper validates local speaker workflows, while pyannote/WhisperX make editable speaker turns more credible than perfect names.
Rationale
"Who said what" is often more useful than exact transcript wording. A humble correction-first speaker layer improves transcript reading, summaries, Ask, and export without damaging trust.
Downsides
True diarization adds model, packaging, performance, and privacy complexity. The product should stage this carefully: editable turns first, local diarization only after the correction workflow is strong.
5

Idea 5. Trust You Can Inspect

Confidence 83% Complexity Medium Trust
Description
Turn privacy and readiness into product UX: what stays local, what analytics excludes, which permissions are active, what each permission unlocks, and what leaves the Mac when the user copies or exports.
Axis
Consumer UX, menu bar, main window, note UI, settings, and trust.
Basis
direct: Cadence already has local audio/transcript storage, optional sanitized analytics, minimal trust copy, and repeated permission surfaces. external: recent Granola privacy-default criticism makes inspectable trust commercially relevant, not just morally nice.
Rationale
Local-first is only a differentiator if users can feel it in tense moments: first launch, Screen Recording prompts, active recording, export, and error recovery.
Downsides
There is a risk of building a verbose settings page. The UX has to be calm and situational: readiness and privacy at the point of concern, deeper detail only when asked.
6

Idea 6. Final Pass as Challenge, Not Replacement

Confidence 81% Complexity Medium Transcript
Description
Make live draft and final pass lineage visible. Instead of a mysterious overwrite, show retained live text, final-pass outcome, replacement boundaries, failure states, and user-visible conflict or confidence cues when the saved-audio pass disagrees.
Axis
Capture reliability and transcription quality.
Basis
direct: The model already distinguishes live draft, finalizing, final, origin, recording ID, empty final failures, retained text, and replacement only for matching recordings.
Rationale
Cadence has the correctness model. Surfacing it would turn a subtle invariant into user trust, especially after the user's earlier live-transcript duplication and stuck-finalization problems.
Downsides
Too much lineage UI can feel technical. The design should show a simple state by default and reveal details only when the final pass fails, changes materially, or needs a retry.
7

Idea 7. Capture Rehearsal

Confidence 70% Complexity Medium Onboarding
Description
Before the first real meeting, let users run a short private rehearsal: capture a few seconds of mic or system audio, show what was heard, show what stayed local, and identify the exact missing permission or capture problem.
Axis
Distribution, privacy, onboarding, permissions, packaging, release readiness.
Basis
direct: The repo has permission panes, source-specific troubleshooting, contextual Screen Recording permission, and audio smoke verification in scripts. reasoned: permissions become less frightening when the app proves the capture path privately before a real call.
Rationale
This is the most practical way to make first-run trust tangible. It also gives Cadence a user-facing version of its own audio smoke test.
Downsides
The verifier flagged the initial version as two ideas stapled together. Keep this focused on rehearsal; release signing/notarization is still important, but belongs to shipping hygiene rather than this product idea.
Cut List

Rejection Summary

# Idea Reason Rejected
1 Cadence Memory / Private Conversation Library Cross-note search and global Ask already exist, so the new premise is thinner than the framing suggests. Keep it as a later extension of Private AI Notepad.
2 Quality Budget Instead of Model Picker Directionally useful, but Cadence already has user-facing quality presets and advanced controls underneath. The novelty delta is too small for the top set.
3 DVR Season Pass for Meetings Recurring capture is an interesting calendar variant, but recurring-rule machinery is not directly supported by the current repo evidence.
4 Ad Hoc Call Capture Manual ad hoc capture fits the product, but detecting likely non-calendar calls has weak evidence and a harder macOS inference surface than the idea admitted.
5 Chain-of-Custody Export / No Share Links by Design The local export and no-cloud stance are strong, but provenance receipts overclaim the current evidence. Fold the trust angle into Trust You Can Inspect.
6 Native Personal Capture Suite Useful as an umbrella thesis, but too broad as a next product idea. The ranked set decomposes it into buildable directions.
7 Ambient Dictation Autopilot Good future direction for dictation, but this run's strongest evidence points to meeting trust and capture recovery first.
